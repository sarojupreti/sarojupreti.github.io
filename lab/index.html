Hattiesburg, MS · 2017 – 2021

William Carey University

Where it started: a degree in mathematics and chemistry.

My first research project was a neural network that predicted the atomization energies of inorganic compounds.

Train a tiny neural network

Unlocked: Neural net

What really happened

That network was my first taste of letting a model find structure in chemistry. I also ran the International Students’ Organization here and left with the University Scholar Award.

Hattiesburg, MS · 2022 – now

Southern Miss · Gu Research Group

For my Ph.D. in polymer science I needed to know how processing changes a film’s structure. That takes far more samples than anyone can make by hand.

So I built robots to make them.

Run the sample line

Unlocked: Robot sidekick

What really happened

The robots dispense, mix, spin-coat and rack hundreds of thin-film samples in hours. Zoom into one of those films to see what we were after.

The robotic platform →

Upton, NY · 2024 – 2025

NSLS-II · Brookhaven Lab

A synchrotron: a ring of electrons that makes X-rays bright enough to see structure a few nanometers across.

Beamtime is short, so every measurement counts.

Find where order disappears

Unlocked: Beamline shades

What really happened

Over 25+ shifts at beamline 11-BM, we measured whole robot-made libraries and built the first high-throughput map of the order–disorder transition in triblock copolymer–homopolymer blends. I wrote several of the beamtime proposals and was experiment lead on them.

Read the case study →

Berkeley, CA · 2024 – now

Advanced Light Source · Beamline 7.3.3

Here the model gets to drive.

It reads every scattering pattern as it lands and picks the next sample itself.

Hit a 30 nm target

Unlocked: Flannel

What really happened

The closed loop reached a 30 ± 1 nm target domain spacing in 20–30 experiments, where a grid scan would need thousands. We commissioned autonomous experimentation at this endstation over 40+ shifts.
