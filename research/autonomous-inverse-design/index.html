Research/Autonomous inverse design

Closing the loop on block copolymer inverse design

A team-built closed loop in which a Gaussian-process optimizer drives a synchrotron beamline directly, finding the processing route to a named target morphology in 20–30 experiments instead of thousands.

The problem

Inverse design starts from the structure you need — here a 30 nm domain spacing, highly ordered, in a block copolymer blend — and asks which processing conditions produce it. Five processing knobs at ten settings each is 100,000 samples; no beamtime allocation covers a grid scan of that. The question: can the experiment choose what to run next, well enough to find the answer before the beamtime ends?

What we built

This was a collaborative effort with computational scientists and beamline staff at the Advanced Light Source. Together we connected three pieces end to end:

  1. Automated GISAXS reduction at beamline 7.3.3, turning raw frames into domain spacing and a measure of order before the next sample is in position.
  2. A Gaussian-process surrogate built on gpCAM, whose uncertainty tells the optimizer where it has measured versus where it is only predicting.
  3. An acquisition step that proposes the next condition and dispatches it to the robotic sample preparation.

The result

For poly(styrene-b-2-vinylpyridine) (S2VP) blends, with a target of 30 ± 1 nm domain spacing and maximized order, the loop converged in 20 to 30 samples — roughly two orders of magnitude fewer than uniform sampling would need.

Nothing in the loop is specific to S2VP: it works over processing parameters and reduced structural descriptors, so it transfers to any system that can be made robotically and measured quickly. It is the foundation of the self-driving lab now being built.
