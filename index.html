/ how a self-driving lab works

Closing the loop

Each stage hands off to the next, and the model chooses what to try. Watch it run, or click through yourself.

MakeMeasureRapid analysisLearnDesign / Command↺

01 Make — Formulate and coat

The robot mixes formulations, coats them onto wafers and racks them for measurement, sample after sample.

The formulation and coating robot, sped up 20×. The robotic platform →

02 Measure — Read the nanostructure

Grazing-incidence X-ray scattering turns each film’s nanoscale order into a 2D pattern.

How GISAXS works: a shallow beam probes the film; the detector records how its nanostructure scatters. Beamline campaigns →

03 Rapid analysis — Reduce and fit as data arrive

NOVA reduces each new detector image, fits its scattering peaks and records the results as measurements come in.

NOVA working through masking, reduction, peak fitting and automated monitoring, sped up 16×. About NOVA →

04 Learn — Update the model

Each round of results reshapes the model’s picture of the design space and sharpens where to look next.

The model’s predicted q₂/q₁ surface over eleven iterations; stars mark each round’s proposed compositions. Learning from the data →

05 Design / Command — Choose and send the next batch

The updated model proposes the next batch — where the prediction looks promising, or where it is least certain — and sends it to the robot to start the next round.

An illustration of how a Gaussian-process model picks the next batch. Bayesian optimization in practice →

    02. Robotic grid-search platform

    Hundreds of thin-film samples fabricated in hours, measured by synchrotron scattering, and turned into the first high-throughput map of the order–disorder transition landscape in triblock copolymer–homopolymer blends.

    03. ML processing–structure
